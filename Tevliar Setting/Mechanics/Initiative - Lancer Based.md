Initiative in this system is not rolled, instead players and monsters will take turns choosing who goes in a round.

To decide who's team goes first in the initiative order each side chooses one character to roll off, adding any initiative bonuses the creatures have. This occurs at the start of the first round, but after any occurring surprise round.

The winner of the roll off earns the first turn of combat for their side. The creature (or another allied creature) takes their turn as usual, followed by an opposing creatures turn, and the turn order alternates back and forth. 

If one side has more characters then the other then once one side is out of characters the other side may take turns for all remaining characters. Once all characters haven taken a turn the round ends and the next round begins. Whichever side had more characters (usually the side that took the last turn in the prior round) goes second in the new round. Characters whom are unconscious may not be declared for a turn.

### Oh no there's 20 goblins and 4 players
Oh no, as forementioned there's 20 goblins but only 4 players. That throws this initiative system for a little bit of a loop (as it was designed for a system where sides are usually roughly equal). A solution that you, presumably an aspiring DM (or an experienced one looking for easy notes), may have already thought of is grouping similar creatures together.

In the above case if each turn 5 goblins are chosen to go instead of the regular 1 character it still resembles normal DnD 5e initiative pretty well. It even still deals the issue of none of your goblins (because I know for a fact you don't roll 20 initiatives unless you're a psycho) rolling above a 5 and having them all go essentially on the same initiative anyways because only the barbarian rolled lower then any of them.

This does require DM discretion and use of best judgement. Maybe grouping 3 hobgoblins per initiative is Ok because you want your two named bugbears and 10 goblins to take the other 4 slots. This also creates an effect of cooperation if you pick creatures near each other to form a sort of squad. Three hobgoblins would probably move together with military precision, so it makes sense for them to share an initiative grouping. For the goblins you'd probably just pick five from the swarm at more or less random, they just happened to act at the same time. 

Mixing creatures/characters is trickier to justify. Although hobgoblins may command a group of goblins to act with them, it's far less likely for a bugbear to do the same. It's ok to leave a bundle to act after all the players have gone, it lets them strategize a bit while you ask for their AC for the 50th time.

### But all of my players took the Alert feat and my rogue really wants to go first in initiative
Alright fine I'll cover a few POTENTIAL adjustments that may satiate the players lust for metagaming. Depending on the edition you're using rules may shift and be different. This system does make quite a few obsolete and since I am absolutely not covering all of them here I'll go over a few common traits/abilities that should be revised.

- Dexterity modifier and Bonuses
	First off with the obvious. Having a high initiative bonus allows you to win the roll off which is the only thing initiative bonus's are actually good for in DnD 5e because after the first round turns become circular, assuming players are killing and not being killed each turn a player takes is the same length away (or shorter) to their next as it was their last. Does the barbarian necessarily deserve to go first? Nah, but the rogue has a really fast response time (and averages consistently over 20) and wins the roll off, shouting a command/warning to the barbarian allowing them to react a little quicker. It gets hazy when you remember that each combat round happens in the same 6 seconds and all the turns technically overlap but that's an issue with the structure of DnD itself. 
- Feats (Alert specifically)
	In the 2024 rules for the Alert feat "A player may swap initiatives with a willing ally".
	You can pretty clearly see why that doesn't work for this system. Keep it simple, instead of swapping the player may take their turn outside of the initiative order. They don't have to wait for an alternation, they can go before any other creature, after they have been declared. They may do this once per combat (combat is defined by the DM) You the DM just declared Bjorn the child eater to take his turn? Too bad Bames Nond the rogue has decided he wants to double up his turn with his buddy who just went and go before Bjorn. Does this throw off the balance of combat? Maybe but I don't care because I've read the source books and there is no balance. Just make the combat harder? Also remember, anything the players can do, a monster can too. >:3
- Advantage on Initiative
	Ok they have advantage during the roll off? Seems pretty simple. If you really need to give them something more then figure something out.
- Initiative 20 (Lair actions and a few niche rules)
	When does initiative count 20 take place in this system? Actually a decent question, it can be ruled as the start of each round. 
- until your next turn effects
	Well what if I end the effect early by taking my turn first? Awesome, you had to end your turn though and if you managed to convince the four other players who were chomping at the bit that it was worth letting Gilgorath the Unfathomable go before all of them so you could see a lil better then good for you. If you (the DM) really want to maintain a bit of balance you could rule these effects as ending "At the end of the next round" instead. Just be prepared for all the dice rolling for 7 different effects at the same time from each player.
- Bleeding out players
	This also works for players whom are bleeding out. They may make their death saves at the end of each round as opposed to being part of the initiative order. This includes the round they are dropped in if they haven't taken their turn yet, otherwise it starts at the end of the next round.
